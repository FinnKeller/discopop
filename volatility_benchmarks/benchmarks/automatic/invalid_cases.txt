Invalid cases
=============

Validation run: autotuner.sh, REPEAT_COUNT=100,
WRITE_SAMPLE_BATCH in {64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048},
DP_NAIVE_SAMPLING=1 against a DP_NAIVE_SAMPLING=0 baseline.

Validity criterion:
  case_*_yes  is valid if it is reported VOLATILE for at least one batch size.
  case_*_no   is valid if it is reported STABLE for every batch size.

The cases listed below did not produce their intended volatility. As required by
the generator prompt they are recorded here and were NOT modified, retuned or
rewritten to make them appear valid. The measured per batch verdicts are quoted
verbatim from benchmarks/automatic/volatility_results/.

Result: 26 of 100 cases are invalid - 5 of 75 case_*_yes and 21 of 25 case_*_no.

A) Intended volatility, none observed
   (5 cases, case_*_yes reported STABLE for every batch size)
-------------------------------------------------------------

Cause: the volatile end has only a few candidate instructions, each of them
taken in a large share of the 100 repetitions, and each candidate touches its
address exactly once. The union of dependencies over all repetitions therefore
survives every sampling window and no distinct edge is ever lost. This is the
known weakness of a uniform rand() % N selection: with N small and 100
repetitions, every variant is observed many times.

B) Intended no volatility, volatility observed
   (21 cases, case_*_no reported VOLATILE for at least one batch size)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

B1) Pure additions - 20 cases.

These cases never lose a dependency; sampling ADDS dependencies. When the
shadow memory is cleared at an on-to-off window transition the runtime forgets
the previous write to an address. The next write to that address is then
emitted as an extra WAW edge, and the following access can be reclassified as
INIT. Verified per key and per dependency segment: every segment reported as
LOST also occurs in the corresponding ADDED record, so the sampled dependency
set is a strict superset of the baseline. The artifact appears as soon as the
total access count of the program exceeds the batch size, which is why these
cases become STABLE again at larger batch sizes.

Every case listed here writes at least one address more than once per
repetition, which is the precondition for the artifact. For reference, the
four case_*_no cases that stayed STABLE everywhere (006, 013, 023, 024) and
the hand checked no volatility references (manual_checked/case_0, case_4) do
not have that property.

B2) Genuine dependency loss - 1 case.

  case_018_none_no
      64: VOLATILE
      128: VOLATILE
      256: VOLATILE
      512: VOLATILE
      1024: VOLATILE
      2048: VOLATILE

  In addition to the added WAW and INIT edges of group B1, this case really
  loses dependencies: at batch 64 nine keys lose WAR segments on the heap
  buffer, for example 'WAR 66@64|GEPRESULT_buffer' at key 48@112. With 64
  heap elements filled and scanned per repetition the access volume is high
  enough that whole write/read pairs fall into off windows, so the intended
  no volatility property does not hold at any tested batch size.
